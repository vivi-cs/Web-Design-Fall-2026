Rabbit Care

Basic Info

Rabbits are extremely delicate creatures. They require a lot more special attention than pets such as cats or dogs. They need plenty of space to run around, as their skeletons are extremely fragile. This means that their ideal environment is a home in which they can free roam and have the space they need in order to exercise their legs.

Diet

Rabbits' diets consist primarily of hay. Rabbit teeth never actually stop growing, so having the hay to chew on helps keep them short and prevents dental pain. Hay also contains a lot of beneficial fibers that they need. The next highest amount of their diet is vegetables; namely leafy greens such as romaine lettuce, (this is one of the only types of lettuce they can eat.) kale, or parsley. Next, rabbits want pellets, this should take up about 5% of their total diet. As they grow older, they need less pellets. Finally, fruits and treats. Given how high in sugar and other unhealthy contents these contain, they should be given sparingly and in small amounts (e.g. 1 slice of banana or one small baby carrot) Contrary to the media, rabbits actually cannot consume carrots in high quantity due to the sugar content.

Medical Care

 Rabbits are special pets. If they get sick or hurt, they need to see an exotic pets veterinarian as opposed to a regular one. Rabbits have a pretty fragile immune system, and exotic vets help make sure that your rabbit is healthy.

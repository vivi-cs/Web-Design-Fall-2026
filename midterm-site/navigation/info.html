Rabbit Info

This section details scientific information about rabbits.

All information is taken from the official Wikipedia article on rabbits, found here.


Rabbits, or less formally known as bunnies, are small, herbivoric prey animals. They fall under the Leporidae family, and are classified as mammals. The most commonly known species of rabbit fall under the Syvilagus genera, including the wild cottontail. Prior to 1912, rabbits and hares were classified as rodents, until they were categorized into Leporidae alongside other mammals like the pika. While often confused for the other, rabbits and hares are notably different species, especially in appearance; rabbits tend to be smaller, rounder, with more small, petite features like their legs, Hares are more slender and lanky, and tend to be taller than their rabbit counterparts.

Due to how a rabbit's eyes are placed on its face, they have almost full 360 degree vision. However, they have a blind spot right between their eyes, on the bridge of their nose. Their whiskers are their primary source for navigating this blind spot.

The rabbit's hind legs are specifically engineered to suit their prey animal lifestyle. They allow the rabbit to jump high, and run at nimble speeds in order to lose a predator.
